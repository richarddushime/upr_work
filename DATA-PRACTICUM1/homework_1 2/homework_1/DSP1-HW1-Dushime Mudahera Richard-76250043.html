Task 1.3 Summarise

Create a summary dataframe that has the mean, median, standard deviation, and interquartile range of the scores for each treatment group. Call it: creativity.sum. Show the summary table as output. Make sure the visual output is rounded to no more than 2 decimal places, but that the data retains the full value.

creativity.sum <- creativity.d %>%
  group_by(Treatment) %>%
  summarise(
    Mean = mean(Score),
    Median = median(Score),
    SD = sd(Score),
    IQR = IQR(Score)
  ) %>%
  mutate(across(where(is.numeric), ~ round(., 2)))

Task 2.1

In your own words, what is pseudoreplication?

Pseudoreplication is when an experiment uses data points that are treated as independent replicates even though they are not really independent. In other words, it happens when the same experimental unit is measured multiple times or when samples are taken from within one treatment group or location but analyzed as if they came from different, independent treatments. This leads to incorrect statistical conclusions because the variation being measured doesn’t actually represent real differences between treatments it reflects repeated measures or local variation within a single unit rather than true replication.

Give an example of a situation where pseudoreplication may occur in an experiment - not from the paper or what we discussed in class. Please note: I will ask students for a few examples in class.

I remember back home in my village around 2011(i dont remember the exact year), when fertilizer was first introduced for Irish potato cultivation. Before that, we did not use any fertilizers. To test whether the fertilizer could increase yield, some farmers divided a single garden into two parts applying fertilizer on one side and leaving the other side untreated to compare the results at harvest time.

I think This situation represents pseudoreplication because both sides of the same garden share the same soil type, rainfall, and environmental conditions. The two sides are not truly independent experimental units. Any difference in yield might be influenced by local variation within the same plot rather than the fertilizer itself. To avoid pseudoreplication, the experiment would need several separate gardens (each treated or untreated independently) to provide true replication.
